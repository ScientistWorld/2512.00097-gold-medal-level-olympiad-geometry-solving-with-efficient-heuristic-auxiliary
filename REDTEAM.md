**The gym does not reliably reward better research.** The reproduced heuristic loses to its random baseline on the hidden test (4/6 versus 5/6), reversing the paper's reported ordering. Removing auxiliary search destroys the controlled auxiliary-construction result, yet preserves all hidden-test solves and earns a higher rank through lower cost. A simple construction-enumeration method solves all six hidden problems: this test is too small to establish the paper's search-efficiency claim. Genuine improvement can score higher, but shortcuts also beat or tie the reference.

Material departures from the paper, based on the local [paper](paper/2512.00097.md), §§4–5, and the supplied implementation:

- The gym uses upstream AlphaGeometry DDAR, rather than the paper's optimized engine with identical-point support, modified deduction rules, and matrix-based algebraic optimizations. Its identical-point candidate generator cannot supply the missing engine support; the code acknowledges that DDAR can reject those candidates.
- The problem representation is AlphaGeometry's point-construction language, rather than the paper's richer geometric-object language. The full HAGeo-409 benchmark and IMO-30 experiment are absent from scoring.
- Extension settings are K=64, N=2, T=5 seconds, compared with the paper's K=2048/8192, N=6, and 60 seconds per DDAR call; the paper used a 64-core machine. The core is one constructed sanity case at T=15. Budget reduction accompanies procedural changes rather than preserving the complete original procedure.
- Candidate pools are truncated, categories interleaved, and attempts use a ranked prefix plus shuffled traversal. Later rounds take the first member of another ranked order. The paper randomly selects constructions from its heuristic pool for N rounds, then runs DDAR on the resulting augmented problem; this implementation checks DDAR after each inserted auxiliary.
- Candidates carry coordinates from one numerical drawing into rebuilt drawings. Some candidates also append numerically observed extra incidences as construction premises. The paper describes selecting a defining construction because it has a favorable incidence, not accepting that incidental incidence as an additional theorem premise. This is a soundness concern identified in the implementation, not a separately proven invalid-proof shortcut in this audit.
- The gym emits success flags and auxiliary clauses without independent proof verification. The paper reports manual checking of its IMO-30 proofs. No validity metric is emitted despite the briefing's stated requirement.

All hidden IDs and their goals are in the visible dataset. No cached hidden answer or paper access is needed. **What the rank and evaluator actually enforce.** For available metric values, the diagnostic follows

`delta = sum_experiments weight * sum_metrics abs(coefficient) * direction * (candidate - reference) / cohort_std`.

Direction is +1 for `higher_is_better=true`, otherwise -1. Negative coefficient signs do not reverse that direction. Hidden benefit magnitudes are 1 for solve rate, .1 for solved count, .35 for upper-band rate, and .25 for weighted rate. Cost magnitudes are .1 for attempts, .05 each for rounds and timeout, and .5 for neural usage. Solve count and solve rate duplicate the same success signal after standardization. Units cancel in numerator and denominator: arbitrary large values do not create proportionally arbitrary large rank changes. They can still create a high-scoring invalid attempt and change every other attempt's normalization, as the impossible-metadata shortcut demonstrates. Future rankings have an unknown cohort, so raw dominance and missing-value ties are stronger conclusions than any quoted scalar diagnostic.

Task failure is not a hard gate. There is no proof-validity check or non-neural/resource compliance gate, only self-reported fields entering a weighted sum. A no-proof constant output dominates the reference. An entirely empty method ties it. The all-failed impossible-cost run stayed below reference in the observed cohort; that is evidence for this run, not a guarantee that arbitrary future normalization or constraint violations cannot compensate for task failure.

Missing values are not replaced with worst values for every metric and experiment. Empty files skip a method; missing optional data skip an experiment. Partial coverage and null costs raise exceptions before writing any new JSON. The test uses the train score file's method registry whenever it is nonempty, so a later-added method on disk is ignored. Direct writes to `scores_test.json` are overwritten on a successful evaluation, but the upstream success flags, difficulty values, and cost declarations are trusted. Statements, goals, proof certificates, and measured work are not bound to the scores.

For the next gym iteration, the necessary changes are to restore immutable task inputs, verify actual proofs against those inputs, measure or enforce resource constraints independently, validate finite bounded fields, score a complete method registry, and emit explicit worst results for failures or missing data. The dataset also needs harder representative problems on which the heuristic beats the random baseline, with the auxiliary core included if it remains a target. These are recommendations only; the evaluator and gym were not patched during this audit.
